# Public Complaints & Grievance Redressal - Records & Compliance Department

## 1. Overview
- **Service Description**: A structured and legally mandated mechanism enabling citizens to formally lodge complaints, report public nuisances, highlight municipal infrastructure failures, appeal against administrative delays, report staff misconduct, and seek redress for service deficiencies. This mechanism ensures accountability through documented tracking, investigation, and resolution within prescribed timelines under the Citizen Charter framework.
- **Governing Laws & Directives**:
    - Right to Information (RTI) Act No. 12 of 2016.
    - Public Administration Circulars on Citizen Charters and Public Grievance Handling (issued by the Ministry of Public Administration and Home Affairs).
    - Municipal Councils Ordinance (Cap. 252) / Urban Councils Ordinance (Cap. 255) / Pradeshiya Sabha Act No. 15 of 1987.
    - Local Authority By-laws on Nuisance, Trade, and Public Health.
    - Parliamentary Commissioner for Administration (Ombudsman) Act No. 17 of 1981.

## 2. Scope of Serviceable Grievances
- **Municipal & Infrastructure Issues**:
    - Blocked stormwater drains, culverts, and canals causing flooding.
    - Damaged, potholed, or poorly maintained municipal roads and pavements.
    - Non-functional or damaged streetlights (report with pole number).
    - Unauthorized permanent/temporary structures, road encroachments, and illegal hoardings.
    - Water supply disruptions managed by the Local Authority (stand posts, bowsers).
- **Public Health & Environmental Nuisance**:
    - Missed or irregular household/commercial garbage collection.
    - Illegal dumping of solid waste, construction debris, or hazardous material on public/vacant lands.
    - Mosquito breeding sites (stagnant water, discarded containers, blocked drains) posing dengue risk.
    - Noise pollution from commercial establishments, workshops, places of worship, or public events.
    - Stray animal hazards (dog packs, cattle roaming on public roads).
    - Insanitary conditions in food premises, bakeries, or markets.
- **Trade & Administrative Complaints**:
    - Illegal business operations without valid Trade License or Environmental Protection License (EPL).
    - Violation of trade license conditions (operating beyond permitted hours, unauthorized change of use).
    - Excessive delays in processing applications for permits, licenses, or plan approvals beyond the stated Citizen Charter SLA.
    - Officer misconduct, negligence, discourtesy, or solicitation of illegal gratification.
    - Non-compliance by the Local Authority with its published Citizen Charter service commitments.

## 3. Required Details & Documentation (Checklist)
To ensure prompt action and verifiable tracking, complainants are requested to provide the following:
- **Complainant Identity**:
    - Full Name (as per NIC).
    - National Identity Card (NIC) Number.
    - Residential Address (within or outside LA limits).
    - Valid Contact Telephone Number (Mobile preferred for SMS updates) and/or Email Address.
    - *Note on Anonymity*: Anonymous complaints are accepted and acted upon for severe public health threats (e.g., active dengue breeding, illegal dumping of hazardous material) or corruption allegations. However, tracking updates will not be available without verified contact details.
- **Incident/Issue Details**:
    - Precise location/address of the issue (including nearest landmark, Grama Niladhari Division, or GPS pin if available).
    - Date and time the problem was first observed.
    - Clear narrative description of the grievance.
- **Supporting Evidence**:
    - Photographs or short video clips clearly depicting the problem (digital evidence is highly encouraged).
    - Previous Complaint Reference Number(s) if this is a recurring or re-opened issue.
    - Copies of any prior written correspondence or receipts submitted to the Local Authority regarding the same matter.

## 4. Submission & Redressal Steps
- **Step 1: Lodging the Complaint** (via any of the following official channels):
    - **In-Person**: Visit the Public Complaints Counter / Citizen Helpdesk during office hours. A designated officer will transcribe or receive the written complaint.
    - **Digital/Email**: Submit via the official Local Authority website portal, dedicated mobile application, or direct email to the Grievance Unit.
    - **Telephone**: Call the Public Hotline / Grievance Unit Helpline. The operator logs the complaint into the system.
    - **Written Letter**: Sent by registered post addressed to the Divisional Secretary/Municipal Commissioner.
    - *RTI Note*: If the complaint pertains to accessing information not available publicly, it must be filed as a formal RTI request under RTI Act No. 12 of 2016 with the applicable fee and designated Information Officer.
- **Step 2: Acknowledgement & Registration**:
    - Upon verification of basic details, the complaint is registered in the Central Grievance Database.
    - A unique **Complaint Reference Number (e.g., COM/LA/2026/01234)** and an Acknowledgement Receipt are generated and issued instantly to the complainant via SMS, Email, or physical slip.
- **Step 3: Triage & Assignment**:
    - The Public Complaints Officer/Compliance Unit reviews the complaint and assigns it to the relevant Subject Division Head:
        - *PHI Division*: Public health nuisances, dengue, food safety.
        - *Technical/Engineering Division*: Roads, drains, streetlights, buildings, water.
        - *Cleansing Division*: Garbage collection, illegal dumping.
        - *Revenue/Trade Division*: Unauthorized trade, license violations.
        - *Internal Audit/Administration*: Staff misconduct, service delays.
- **Step 4: Field Investigation & Inspection**:
    - The assigned officer (e.g., Area PHI, Technical Officer, Overseer, Revenue Inspector) conducts a mandatory site visit within the SLA timeline.
    - The officer verifies the facts, assesses the severity, and photographs the site.
- **Step 5: Action & Enforcement**:
    - **For Public Works**: The officer issues a work order to the relevant crew or contractor for rectification (e.g., drain clearing, pothole patching).
    - **For Nuisance/Encroachment**: A formal **Enforcement Notice** is served to the offending party (e.g., a 7-day notice to abate nuisance under the Municipal Councils Ordinance/By-laws). Failure to comply leads to legal action in the Magistrate's Court.
- **Step 6: Closure & Notification**:
    - Upon completion of remedial action or enforcement, the officer updates the case status in the database as "Resolved" with detailed remarks.
    - The complainant is notified of the resolution via SMS, Email, or formal letter, referencing the original Complaint Reference Number. The complainant is provided a window to file an appeal if dissatisfied with the resolution.

## 5. SLAs, Timelines & Escalation
- **Acknowledgement SLA**:
    - Counter / Hotline / Online Portal: **Immediate** (instant reference number).
    - Email / Written Postal: Within **1 working day** of receipt.
- **Emergency / Priority 1 (Public Health & Life Safety)**:
    - *Examples*: Active dengue breeding points, burst water/sewer mains, road cave-ins, dangerous structures, stray cattle on major roads.
    - *Action Initiation*: Within **24 to 48 hours**.
- **Standard Infrastructure & Nuisance (Priority 2)**:
    - *Examples*: Blocked drains, missed garbage, potholes, illegal dumping, streetlight failure, noise complaint, illegal construction.
    - *Investigation & Initial Response*: Within **5 to 14 working days**. Final resolution timeline communicated based on complexity.
- **Complaint Closure**:
    - Target total resolution: **30 working days** for standard cases.
- **Escalation Process (If Unresolved Within SLA)**:
    - **Level 1**: Escalate to the **Public Complaints Officer / Head of Subject Division**.
    - **Level 2**: Escalate to the **Internal Audit & Compliance Officer** for administrative review.
    - **Level 3**: Formal written appeal to the **Municipal Commissioner / Divisional Secretary**.
    - **Level 4 (External)**: If exhausted within the institution, escalate to the **Parliamentary Commissioner for Administration (Ombudsman)** or the **Human Rights Commission of Sri Lanka** for administrative and procedural injustice. The complainant retains the right to seek legal remedy.
